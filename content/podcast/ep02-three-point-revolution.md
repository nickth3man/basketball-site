## Episode Summary

The NBA added the three-point line in 1979–80, but for most of the next two decades, teams treated it as a novelty. Today, the average team attempts over 35 threes per game. We trace how this transformation happened — and what the data reveals about teams that embraced it early.

### Topics Covered

**Opening (0:00–10:00): The Ignored Line**
In 1979–80, teams averaged 2.8 three-point attempts per game. By 2015–16, that number had risen to 22.4. By 2022–23: 35.1. We examine why adoption was so slow — and who saw the value early.

**The Dallas Mavericks and Beyond (10:00–24:00)**
Don Nelson's "Nellie Ball" in Dallas during the 1990s was one of the first systematic attempts to exploit the three-point line. We look at how roster construction informed shot selection years before analytics departments existed.

**Steph Curry and the GSW Disruption (24:00–40:00)**
The Golden State Warriors' 2015–16 season — 73 wins, 13.1 three-point attempts per game — didn't just set records; it changed what every team in the league aspired to build. We use league-wide shot distribution data to show the inflection point the Warriors created.

**Closing (40:00–48:37): Has Three-Point Volume Peaked?**
With some teams shooting 45+ threes per game, are we approaching a ceiling? We discuss whether the optimization has run its course and what the next efficiency frontier might be.

### Key Statistics Mentioned

- 1980 NBA average: 2.8 3PA per team per game
- 2016 GSW: 31.6 3PA per game (championship season)
- 2023 NBA average: 35.1 3PA per team per game
- Steph Curry career 3P%: 42.7% (all-time record volume + efficiency)
