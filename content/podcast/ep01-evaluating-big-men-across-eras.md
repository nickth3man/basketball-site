## Episode Summary

In our debut episode, we explore how statistical analysis of big men has evolved alongside the game itself. The center position has transformed from a dominant interior force to a versatile playmaking hub — and our metrics have had to keep pace.

### Topics Covered

**Opening (0:00–8:00): The Wilt Problem**
Wilt Chamberlain's 1961–62 season — 50.4 PPG, 25.7 RPG — is so statistically distant from modern norms that it challenges the very framework of cross-era comparison. We discuss what Box Plus/Minus and Win Shares tell us when we try to contextualize Wilt.

**Middle Segment (8:00–32:00): The Hakeem–Shaq–Duncan Triangle**
Three dominant big men, three different peak years (1994, 2000, 2003), three different skill sets. We break down their defensive ratings, usage rates, and the team systems that surrounded them to ask: who was most valuable to their franchise?

**Analytics Deep Dive (32:00–46:00): The Jokic Revolution**
Nikola Jokic's three MVP seasons represent something statistically unprecedented for a center: elite passing, high usage, and exceptional efficiency. His BPM values place him among the five most valuable players per-game in the last decade. We walk through the numbers.

**Closing (46:00–52:14): Listener Questions**
Can a center win the championship as the primary star in today's NBA? Historical precedent and current roster construction trends examined.

### Key Statistics Mentioned

- Chamberlain 1962: 50.4 PPG, 25.7 RPG, 48.5 FG%
- Shaq 2000: 29.7 PPG, 13.6 RPG, 57.4 FG%, 30.6 PER
- Duncan 2003: 23.3 PPG, 12.9 RPG, 57.9 TS%
- Jokic 2022: 27.1 PPG, 13.8 RPG, 7.9 APG, 67.3 TS%
