Every June, teams select the next generation of NBA talent. Some draft classes produce generational stars; others are remembered for their busts. Using career Win Shares, Player Efficiency Rating, and All-Star appearances, we've analyzed the data to identify the greatest draft classes in NBA history.

## Methodology

We evaluated draft classes from 1966 to 2010 (giving recent classes time to mature). Key metrics:
- **Total Career Win Shares** generated by all players from that class
- **Number of Hall of Famers** or likely Hall of Famers
- **All-Star appearances** produced by the class
- **MVP winners** from the class

## 1. The 1984 Draft Class

No analysis of great draft classes can start anywhere else. The 1984 class produced:
- **Michael Jordan** (Pick 3, Bulls) — 6 championships, 5 MVPs, 6 Finals MVPs
- **Hakeem Olajuwon** (Pick 1, Rockets) — 2 championships, 1 MVP
- **Charles Barkley** (Pick 5, 76ers) — 1 MVP, 11 All-Star selections
- **John Stockton** (Pick 16, Jazz) — all-time assists leader

The combined career Win Shares of the 1984 class dwarfs virtually every other year. Jordan alone generated over 200 career Win Shares.

## 2. The 1996 Draft Class

The 1996 class is remembered for one transformative moment: five high school players selected in the first round, headlined by:
- **Allen Iverson** (Pick 1, 76ers) — 1 MVP, 11 All-Star selections
- **Ray Allen** (Pick 5, Bucks) — NBA record 2,973 three-pointers at retirement
- **Kobe Bryant** (Pick 13, Hornets/Lakers) — 5 championships, 1 MVP
- **Steve Nash** (Pick 15, Suns) — 2 MVPs

A remarkable class that redefined how teams valued point guards and perimeter scoring.

## 3. The 2003 Draft Class

The "LeBron class" is potentially the deepest of the modern era:
- **LeBron James** (Pick 1, Cavaliers) — still active; historically dominant
- **Dwyane Wade** (Pick 5, Heat) — 3 championships, 13 All-Star selections
- **Carmelo Anthony** (Pick 3, Nuggets) — 10 All-Star selections
- **Chris Bosh** (Pick 4, Raptors) — 2 championships, 11 All-Star selections

If you include LeBron's ongoing career, the 2003 class may eventually surpass 1984 in total Win Shares.

## Honorable Mentions

- **1979 class**: Magic Johnson (Pick 1), Larry Bird (Pick 6)
- **1969 class**: Kareem Abdul-Jabbar (then Lew Alcindor)
- **2009 class**: Stephen Curry (Pick 7), James Harden (Pick 3), Ricky Rubio, DeMar DeRozan

## The Numbers Tell the Story

Draft class quality correlates strongly with league competitiveness in subsequent decades. The 1979, 1984, 1996, and 2003 classes all preceded eras of extraordinary basketball quality — suggesting that talent concentrations in particular classes reshape entire competitive landscapes.

The data is clear: some generations of players arrive together, and when they do, basketball is never the same.
