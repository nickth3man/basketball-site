If you've browsed NBA box scores, you've seen field goal percentage (FG%) as the standard measure of shooting efficiency. Yet FG% has a fundamental flaw: it treats a three-pointer and a two-pointer as equal, and ignores free throws entirely. True Shooting Percentage (TS%) was developed to fix exactly these blind spots.

## The Formula

True Shooting Percentage is calculated as:

**TS% = Points / (2 × (FGA + 0.44 × FTA))**

The denominator represents "true shooting attempts" — weighting free throw attempts at 44% to account for the fact that most free throw trips are part of a two-shot foul (not always a full "possession" spent).

## Why It Matters

Consider two hypothetical players in the same game:
- **Player A**: 10-of-20 from the field (0 threes, 0 free throws) = 50% FG%, 25 points
- **Player B**: 7-of-14 from the field (3-of-6 on threes, 4-of-5 free throws) = 50% FG%, 25 points

Both have identical field goal percentages. But Player B used *fewer* shot attempts to score the same points, and generated free throws — a vastly more efficient output.

TS% captures this: both players score 25 points, but Player B's denominator (true attempts) is smaller, yielding a higher TS%.

## League Average as Benchmark

The NBA-wide TS% in a typical modern season hovers around **57–58%**. A player above 60% is considered an excellent shooter; below 52% is below-average efficiency.

Elite seasons have featured TS% values above 65% — Rudy Gobert's interior finishing and Stephen Curry's three-point volume both produce these figures in strong seasons.

## Limitations

TS% measures scoring efficiency per shot attempt, but it doesn't weight volume. A backup who scores 8 points on 6 true attempts with a 67% TS% contributes less than a starter who scores 30 points on 27 true attempts with a 56% TS%.

For complete evaluation, pair TS% with volume (points per game, usage rate) to understand the full picture.

## In Practice

When you see a player's box score on NBA Reference, TS% quickly tells you whether their point total came efficiently. A 30-point night on 70% TS% is a masterclass; 30 points on 44% TS% means they needed many more shots to reach that total.

True Shooting Percentage is one of the most information-dense single statistics in basketball analytics — a small number that unlocks large insight.
