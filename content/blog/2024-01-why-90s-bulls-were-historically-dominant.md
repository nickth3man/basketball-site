Few teams in NBA history have captured the imagination of fans and analysts quite like the 1995–96 Chicago Bulls, who finished with a then-record 72 wins. But raw win totals only tell part of the story. To truly understand the Bulls' dominance, we need to dig into the numbers.

## The Defensive Foundation

Before Phil Jackson arrived, the Bulls were a respectable team. After he introduced the Triangle Offense and emphasized defensive intensity, they became something else entirely. During the dynasty years (1991–1998), the Bulls ranked in the top three in defensive rating in five of their six championship seasons.

The 1995–96 Bulls posted a defensive rating of 92.9 — a figure that, adjusted for pace and era, remains one of the finest in league history. Scottie Pippen's ability to guard the opposing team's best player at any position was central to this system.

## Michael Jordan's Offensive Dominance

Jordan averaged 30.1 points per game over his career — the highest ever among players with substantial playing time. But his True Shooting percentage of 56.9% demonstrates he was not merely a volume scorer; he was an efficient one.

During the 72-win season, Jordan posted a PER (Player Efficiency Rating) of 29.0, complemented by a Win Shares total of 20.0. These metrics, when contextualized against his era, place him firmly among the two or three most valuable players in a single season in league history.

## The Triangle Offense: Spacing and Movement

Phil Jackson's Triangle Offense was radical for its time. It demanded basketball IQ from all five players on the floor, created natural spacing, and reduced predictability. Teams could not simply double Jordan because Pippen, Kukoc, or Harper would punish the rotation.

The system allowed Chicago to score efficiently without relying on isolation plays for the entire shot clock — they ranked top-5 in offensive rating in four of their six championship years.

## Historical Context

To place the Bulls in proper context: the 1995–96 team's Simple Rating System (SRS) of +12.26 is the second-highest ever recorded by Basketball Reference, trailing only the 1971–72 Los Angeles Lakers. Their net rating of +12.3 in the 72-win season stands as a towering benchmark.

The dynasty spanned two three-peats (1991–93 and 1996–98), interrupted only by Jordan's first retirement. No team since has replicated consecutive three-peats, making the Bulls uniquely positioned in basketball history.

## Conclusion

Statistics confirm what the eye test suggested at the time: the 1990s Bulls were genuinely special. Their defensive intensity, Jordan's offensive brilliance, and Jackson's system created a convergence of factors that produced one of sport's greatest dynasties. The numbers don't just validate the legacy — they amplify it.
